# What you're allowed to clip

Not legal advice. The aim is to keep the person's accounts safe and their clips approved.

## Safe
- **Campaign footage**: the owner posted it for clippers and set rules in the brief. Follow the brief.
- **Your own gameplay / your own stream.** Still check the game publisher's video policy (below).
- Footage where the creator has **given you permission in writing** (a message or email you can show).

- A friend's stream: get a written OK first (a DM you can screenshot), and let them send you the file
  rather than ripping it from Twitch/YouTube, whose terms usually forbid downloading other people's videos.

## Not safe
- Re-uploading other streamers' or YouTubers' videos without permission ("I just cut it" is not permission).
  Platforms remove these and give strikes; campaigns reject them.
- Official trailers and cutscenes used as the whole clip. Publishers' Content ID claims them.
- Music: in-game radio songs and licensed tracks get claimed or muted. **Turn the in-game radio off** while recording,
  or mute that section.
- Story **spoilers**, especially in the first weeks after a game launches. Many publishers ask creators to avoid them,
  and audiences report them.

## GTA 6 specifically (Rockstar)
- Rockstar's policy on posting its copyrighted material is written for **occasional, non-commercial fan content**,
  restricts spoilers, and allows cutscenes only as part of a longer play-through or narrated video.
  Read the current page: support.rockstargames.com (search "Policy on posting copyrighted Rockstar Games material").
- Earning ad money from GTA footage is a grey area under that policy. Campaign payments for clipping a streamer's
  footage come from the streamer/brand, but the game content is still Rockstar's: follow both the brief and the policy.
- GTA 6 launched on 19 November 2026 on PS5 and Xbox Series X|S.

## If unsure
Ask the footage owner, or pick different footage. One strike can cost more than a month of clips earns.
